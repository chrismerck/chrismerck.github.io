I recently stumbled on [Kato Lomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kat%C3%B3_Lomb)'s book *[How I Learn Languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/How_I_Learn_Languages)* and found her methodology compelling for [second language acquisition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second-language_acquisition). While it is not a complete solution, I recommend it to any aspiring language learner.

<!-- more -->

## Extensive Reading

The core of Lomb's method is extensive reading, even when comprehension falls below the commonly recommended 98% lexical threshold. She writes:

> Ignore what you can't immediately understand. If a word is important, it will occur several times and explain itself anyway. Base your progress on the known, not the unknown.

She emphasizes building a personal relationship with the material through marginal annotations, gaining deeper understanding through personal effort than automatic dictionary consultation, and experiencing achievement through self-directed problem-solving.

## Overcoming the Linguistic Deadlock

When learners encounter overwhelming texts, Lomb introduces the "push-pull" concept: difficult material (push) combined with genuinely engaging content (pull) helps learners persevere.

> We must admit that in a foreign language in which we have a deficient vocabulary, reading can be boring... We need something more to help us get through it. That something is the pull of a truly interesting text.

Hungarian writer [Dezso Kosztolanyi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dezs%C5%91_Kosztol%C3%A1nyi) captured this sentiment poetically in his account of learning Portuguese through a novel:

> An exciting game, a coquettish hide-and-seek, a magnificent flirt with the spirit of humanity... This is the elixir of my life.

## Books as Learning Tools

Lomb expresses profound appreciation for books as the most accessible means for creating a personalized language learning environment:

> A book can be pocketed and discarded, scrawled and torn into pages... Its content lures you for intellectual adventures...

She views books as eternal companions: when you grow out of one, you simply discard it for another.

## Autologue (Self-Conversation)

Lomb recommends conversing with oneself, which I practice during walks to identify vocabulary gaps. This technique of running an internal [monologue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internal_monologue) applies beyond language learning to subjects like philosophy.

## The Role of Teachers and Technology

While Lomb valued teacher-student relationships for slowed speech delivery, I would argue that modern technology achieves similar effects. She writes:

> To return to my method of language study, what I expect from my Azilian teacher is what I cannot get from either books or the radio. Firstly, I ask the teacher to speak at a slower than average speed...

Recommended audio software for slowing speech includes:

- iPhone: Amazing Slow Downer
- Windows: Windows Media Player 10
- Alternatives: [Audacity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audacity_(audio_editor)), iTunes (though with lower sound quality)

## Delayed Consolidation

Lomb notes a psychological phenomenon where language knowledge sometimes shows delayed consolidation, comparing it to musical conductors who practice intensely then rest before performances:

> A certain sedimentation does no harm to language knowledge, just as with wine... In language learning, the amount of language learned while abroad will often not show up until well after arriving home.

## Next Steps

I have remaining questions about Lomb's concepts of "interpolation" versus "extrapolation" during reading, but I am committed to experimenting with her methods. I plan to start by reading Grimm's Fairytales in Chinese (格林童话).
