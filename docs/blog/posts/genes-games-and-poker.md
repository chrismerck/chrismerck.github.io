I've been reading Dawkins' *[The Selfish Gene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Selfish_Gene).* What I love most about the book is how my mind is just overflowing with genetic systems, simulatable quantitative machines; I'm just dying to code up some simulations of the systems he exposes so eloquently in the book. Every few pages, a new system comes into view. I have to pause, just stare into the void as my mind runs fast, imagining the mathematics needed to construct from the theory a working computer model.

<!-- more -->

This reading has led me to do a little more in-depth reading on [game theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Game_theory), in particular, the graduate Game Theory course from the mathematics section of MIT's [OpenCourseWare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT_OpenCourseWare). There, you find the polar opposite of Dawkins' readable eloquence. Slides of precise definitions, theorems, sparingly annotated. But the mathematics are intriguing.

So, for the todo list (to be done simultaneously):

* Serious math-based game theory course. Learn the good stuff. [Von Neumann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_von_Neumann), [Nash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Forbes_Nash_Jr.), et al. Doubles as math workout.
* Implement simulations of several of Dawkins' illuminating genetic systems. Doubles as a Python learning experience.
* What does this have to do with poker?

Bring it on.
