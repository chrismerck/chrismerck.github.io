The history of English fascinates me. Here follows my very brief but hopefully reasonably factual account.

<!-- more -->

**before 43 AD:** England inhabited by the [Celts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celts). Various [Celtic languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celtic_languages) are spoken in the Isles.

**43 AD:** Romans invade and occupy England. [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin) is used for administrative purposes.

**~400 AD:** Various [Germanic tribes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germanic_peoples) invade (collectively known as the [Anglo-Saxons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Saxons)) and eventually expel the Romans. [Old English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_English) (a.k.a. Anglo-Saxon) develops from the variants of Old German spoken by the invaders.

**~600 AD:** Christian missions to England introduce a wave of Latin and Greek loan words.

**~900 AD:** [Vikings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vikings) invade and cohabit with Anglo-Saxons in England. Many words are loaned from their language ([Old Norse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Norse)). [*Beowulf*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beowulf) is composed around this period.

**1066 AD:** The [Normans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normans) (from France) invade and conquer England. Their language (the Norman variety of [Old French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_French)) is used in the courts and in educated circles for 300 years.

**1362 AD:** The use of French having declined, King [Edward III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_III_of_England) officially addresses Parliament in English.

**~1500 AD:** Pronunciation of many words changes with the so-called [Great Vowel Shift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Vowel_Shift). [Shakespeare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Shakespeare) lives and writes in what we call [Early Modern English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Modern_English).

**~1650 AD:** Renaissance and industrial revolution. Many words from New Latin and Greek enter the language.

**1755 AD:** The first significant dictionary published. [Modern English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_English) is born.

**1776 AD:** [American revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolution). [American English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_English) starts to officially diverge from British English.
