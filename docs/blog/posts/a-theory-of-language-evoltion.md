My interests in [evolutionary algorithms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evolutionary_algorithm) on one hand and language on the other have led me to ponder the evolution of language.

<!-- more -->

## Terminology

To avoid conflating cultural linguistic systems with biological language capabilities, I'll borrow some German terminology. I use *[Sprache](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/Sprache)* to denote a particular language (such as English or German), and *Faculty* to refer to the innate human capacity for language (in the [Chomskyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_grammar) sense).

## Transmission Mechanisms

The Faculty is inherited by the offspring just as any other genetic traits are: through recombination of the parents' genes.

The Sprache, however, is not present in the genome, and it is not genetically transmitted from parents to offspring. Rather, the Sprache is transmitted *[memetically](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memetics)* to offspring through in-context audible utterances (speech).

## The Neo-Darwinian Analogy

I propose that languages evolve through a mechanism analogous to [neo-Darwinian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-Darwinism) natural selection. The analogy works at several levels:

| Linguistics | Biology |
|-------------|---------|
| Sprache (language) | Species |
| Speaker | Individual |
| Word | Gene |
| Vocabulary (~20,000 words) | Genome |
| Lexicon | Gene pool |
| Word learning | Gene duplication |
| Sound change across generations | Mutation |
| Word usage | Gene expression |

Words demonstrate communal properties, functioning together in meaningful expressions, much as genes collaborate in biological functions.

## Selection Mechanism

The probability of a word being learned varies with the usage frequency of the word. When a person makes a speech act, he chooses to use words which best convey his meaning. This creates a [positive feedback loop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positive_feedback): useful words are used more frequently, which increases the probability that they are learned by others, which increases their frequency further.

Speakers also tend to resist borrowing from other Sprachen, paralleling [reproductive isolation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reproductive_isolation) between biological species.

## Future Directions

Several paths for investigation present themselves:

1. Test where the analogy fits and where it fails
2. Apply synthetic neo-Darwinian tools to linguistics
3. Seek empirical evidence for the proposed selection mechanism
4. Develop computational simulations and mathematical models
5. Consult the existing literature on [language evolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Origin_of_language)
6. Connect findings to the relationship between Faculty and Sprache
